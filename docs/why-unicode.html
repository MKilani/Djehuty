Background

Why Unicode?

Hieroglyphs as text, rather than as images.

Unicode hieroglyphs transcribe Egyptian signs as strings of text. Tools such as JSesh generate images, which can be accurate but cannot be searched, copied or stored as text. A Unicode hieroglyph can be inserted into any editor, manipulated like any other character, found with a search function, used in databases and tables, and kept searchable in a PDF.

What they are for

Unicode hieroglyphs are limited by the font that displays them, so they are not suited to palaeographically accurate copies of inscriptions. They are meant to transcribe texts in a uniform way: when a text is quoted and discussed for its literary or linguistic content, rather than for its appearance on the support.

The distinction is familiar from manuscript studies. A palaeographical copy reproduces each scribal peculiarity of form. A content-wise transcription, set in a standard typeface, serves the critical edition. Unicode hieroglyphs are meant for the Egyptological equivalent of the second.
