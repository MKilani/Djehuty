How the font works

Quadrats and ligatures

Why signs are typed in order and grouped by the font.

The problem

In actual texts, hieroglyphs are usually distributed into quadrats: virtual rectangles or squares that organise the signs in space. The sign group htp is commonly set as a single quadrat, not as a line.

Ligatures

The font does this through ligatures: substitutions stored in the font itself. Typography offers familiar examples. The English sequence f + i is set as a single ligature, and in Devanagari the sequence ह + म becomes one conjunct.

EgyptianHiero works the same way. When it meets a sequence of signs that matches one of its ligatures, it arranges them as the corresponding quadrat.

What follows

  • No positional markers. Signs are typed one after the other. Unlike Manuel de Codage or JSesh, no characters such as :, * or - are needed.
  • Search still works. A quadrat still corresponds to its sequence, so search finds signs inside quadrats, and sequences that span several quadrats.
  • Only encoded quadrats. The font displays only the quadrats it encodes; custom quadrats cannot be built. Version 9.03 encodes about 10,600 ligatures, and new ones are added with each release. Requests for additional quadrats are welcome.
